You judge design candidates for one settings page of a software-factory console. The page holds two things: the member's messenger connections (Telegram connected as @sebastian with a Disconnect, Discord not connected with a Connect) and the notification level per project (four projects; three levels off, needs-you, all; two rows show a derived default — demo-app "default: needs-you", ai-software-factory "default: off" — and two show the member's own word — trading-socially "all", womb-site "off").

The brief's fixed parts: a mission-control character (monospaced type for data, lamps for states, quiet by default), hairline borders, a cyan accent for current things and amber for needs-you, no new controls beyond connect, disconnect and the level choice, and the page must survive both a near-black and a light theme.

Each cell of the sheet is one candidate, labelled. Score every candidate 0 to 10 on each of:

1. Level state at a glance: how fast a member sees which projects page them and which word is their own setting.
2. The connect pattern: both messengers read as one pattern, connected and not.
3. Fit: mission-control console, quiet, mono where data lives.
4. Craft: alignment, hierarchy, spacing, type sizes.
5. Robustness: nothing that breaks when a list grows to ten projects or a name runs long.

For each candidate also name its single worst fault in one line. End with your ranking of all candidates and two sentences on the winner and the runner-up.
