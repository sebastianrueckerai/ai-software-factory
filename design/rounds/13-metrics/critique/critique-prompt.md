You are judging six design directions for one page of a web console for an
autonomous software factory. The page is the project's metrics page: how
the project ships, how steady its work is, what it costs, how much ran
without a human, and what the factory itself did — over 28 days against the
previous 28. Sebastian picked the information hierarchy in advance: small
capital section labels (DELIVERY, STABILITY, COST, AUTONOMY, THE FACTORY
ITSELF), and per figure a row reading name · value · move · spark, except
direction STRIP, which exists as the comparison: a shared time axis with
weekly marks per family and the numbers in a right column.

Six directions, each shown in dark and light on two contact sheets (the
same six layouts twice). Labels: rows, twin, strip, figures, headline,
free.

Score each direction 0-10 on each question, one line of reason each:

1. Glance: do the sections read in weight order — delivery, stability,
   cost, autonomy, factory — and does "how is this project doing" land in
   one look?
2. The figure row: do value, move and spark read as one fact without
   crowding, with the sparks aligned so the eye can run down them?
3. The deltas: "+4", "−25 m" — do they say their direction clearly, and
   would a reader know up is good for throughput but bad for cost? (The
   colour language must stay quiet: cyan belongs to links and the open
   page's word only.)
4. Text fidelity: name every misspelled, duplicated, dropped or invented
   word or figure you can read. Image models invent text; this is the
   hardest test.
5. Calm: does the page stay a mission-control readout (hairlines, quiet
   ink, no decoration) or does the charting turn loud?

Then rank the six directions best to worst with one sentence each. Be
specific and critical; do not reward prettiness over legibility. Judge the
design, not the image model's rendering artefacts, but DO count unreadable
or wrong text as a defect of the direction.
