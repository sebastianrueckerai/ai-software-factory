You judge design candidates for the top navigation bar of a software-factory console. The bar holds, left to right: a home link ("Factory console"), a project select ("demo" or a long slug like "ai-software-factory-code"), eight destinations (Board, New task, Projects, Resources, Costs, Policy, Alerts, Notifications), and at the right a moon-sun theme toggle, the member's name and Log out. Board is the open page in every candidate. Under the bar sits the board page: a status strip and three columns of task cards — context, not the subject.

The brief's fixed parts: one 44 px bar (a second stacked bar only where the candidate argues it, total chrome at most 88 px), hairline borders, mission-control character (mono for figures, small capitals for labels, lamps for states, quiet by default), cyan for system and current things, amber for needs-you, no new colour, every text pair 4.5:1 or better, and the bar must survive both a near-black and a light theme. The bar must not re-count notifications that other surfaces already count; at most a lamp without a number.

Each cell of the sheet is one candidate, labelled. Score every candidate 0 to 10 on each of:

1. Where you are: how fast a member sees which of the eight pages is open.
2. New task standing apart: the one action reads as an action, without a new colour.
3. Grouping and fit: the eight destinations read as groups (the working board against the reference pages against policy and alerts), and everything fits at 1440 with the long slug left over for the right end.
4. Craft: alignment, hierarchy, spacing, type sizes against a 44 px bar.
5. Robustness: nothing that breaks when the slug runs long, the window narrows to 1024, or Notifications is absent.

For the phone sheets score the same five but read criterion 3 and 5 as: the Menu holds the rest, every target 44 px, Board never hidden behind a second press. For each candidate also name its single worst fault in one line. End with your ranking of all candidates and two sentences on the winner and the runner-up.
