Two fixed candidates for the top navigation bar of a software-factory console meet in a final compare. The bar holds, left to right: a home link ("Factory console"), a project select ("demo"), eight destinations (Board, New task, Projects, Resources, Costs, Policy, Alerts, Notifications), and at the right a moon-sun theme toggle, the member's name and Log out. Board is the open page in every shot.

The brief's fixed parts: one 44 px bar (a second stacked bar only where the candidate argues it, total chrome at most 88 px), hairline borders, mission-control character (mono for figures, small capitals for labels, lamps for states, quiet by default), cyan for system and current things, amber for needs-you, no new colour, every text pair 4.5:1 or better, and both themes must hold.

Candidate A ("sparse", cells A1-A4): quiet small-capitals words with generous space between groups and no boxes; the current page's word carries the cyan and a short underline; "New task" is the bar's only bordered word; on the phone the word "Board" sits in the bar so it needs no second press, and the Menu sheet holds the other destinations as 44 px rows.

Candidate B ("panel", cells B1-B4): the eight links sit in one hairline-framed segmented panel with internal dividers only at group boundaries; the Board segment is filled with the raised colour and its word cyan; "New task" carries a 1 px border inside its segment, the panel's only bordered word; on the phone the Menu opens an anchored sheet in the same segmented language as full-width 44 px rows.

Score each candidate 0 to 10 on:
1. Where you are: how fast a member sees which page is open.
2. New task standing apart as the one action, without a new colour.
3. Grouping and fit at 1440, and on the phone in the Menu sheet.
4. Craft: alignment, hierarchy, spacing, type sizes against a 44 px bar, in both themes.
5. Robustness: nothing that breaks when the slug runs long, the window narrows, or a destination is absent.

Name each candidate's single worst remaining fault in one line. End with your winner and one sentence on why it wins for a member who lives in this console daily, and one sentence on what would make you reverse that verdict.
