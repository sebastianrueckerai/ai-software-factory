You are a design critic for an operations console (a mission-control web
app for an autonomous software factory). The style is settled and pinned
(the production stylesheet: hairlines, small-capitals labels, monospace
tabular figures, cyan only as an accent, amber only for attention). Two
candidates for the compact screen remain.

Candidate A is a bundle, shown in two states: image 1 is the compact
rest state — chat at its 24 rem floor with the thread table beside the
conversation, the composer spanning the chat's full width under both,
the status strip compressed to spend gauges plus one row of lamp chips
(dot · name, detail on hover), and a glyph toggle in the chat head that
grows the chat to the whole screen (Esc or the flipped glyph returns);
image 2 is the same console with a card drawer open over the board —
the drawer carries Overview, Session, Activity, Files and a Chat tab,
so a conversation about the card stays in the card's context.

Candidate B is a single direction: the thread list leaves the side
table and becomes a CHAT | THREADS tab pair in the chat head, giving
the conversation the panel's full width at every split.

Judge:
- Composer room at the compact split: whose long-draft box is roomier
  and calmer to type into?
- Thread glanceability: A keeps the table always beside the
  conversation; B trades that glance for conversation width. Which
  trade serves an operator mid-task better?
- The strip compression in A: do the lamp chips read as instruments,
  or as noise? Is anything lost that the text rows carried?
- The drawer Chat tab in A: does it earn its place, or is it a second
  chat too many?
- Focus and return: full-screen chat and back, drawer open and closed —
  which candidate keeps the operator's place better?

Then: pick ONE candidate for the console, one paragraph of reason, and
name the single change you would make to the winner before building it.

Do not redesign the candidates; judge them.
