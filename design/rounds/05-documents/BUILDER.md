## The queries

`?scenario=busy` and `?theme=dark|light` as the base. The round adds `?doc=<path>`, which opens that document at load (the shots use `doc=docs/roadmap.md`), and `?conflict=1`, which arms a refusal on the next save through `DOCS.conflictNext('docs/roadmap.md')`.

## What every variant shares

The three variants differ in where a document opens and how the member moves between documents, and in nothing else. They share, built the same way:

1. **The document view.** Root element `#doc-view`. Its head is one quiet row: the path in small mono, class `doc-path`; the switch Read | Edit as two small buttons `[data-mode="read"]` and `[data-mode="edit"]` with `aria-pressed`; "Open in Forgejo ↗" as `a.doc-forgejo` (href `#`, title "Forgejo's page of this file, in a new tab"); "New document" as `[data-act="new"]`; and a close × with `aria-label="Close"`. The body in read mode: `DOCS.render(md, path)` inside `article.doc-body`, the text at a measure of 68 to 72 characters (`max-width: 70ch`), the console's text type at the base size, headings in the text colour, body in the text colour, links in the accent colour with an underline on hover and focus, inline code in mono on the raised surface, a fenced block in mono in a panel. The body scrolls inside the view, never the page. The document is a reading surface, not code: no line numbers, no gutter.
2. **Links open in place.** A click on any `[data-doc="<path>"]` (a link in the text, a pin, a tab, a tree row, a crumb, a recent entry) opens that path the variant's way and calls `DOCS.nav.open(path)`. A `doc-missing` link does nothing but show its title. Where the variant has history (back and forward), push `#doc=<path>` on the URL with `history.pushState` and handle `popstate`, so the browser's own back button works, and say so in the report. The scroll position of a document is kept when the member comes back to it.
3. **Edit mode.** `[data-mode="edit"]` swaps the body for `textarea#doc-editor` with the raw Markdown in mono at the base size, the full height of the view, `spellcheck="true"`, and under it one row: "Save as commit" `[data-act="save"]` as the primary button, "Cancel" `[data-act="cancel"]`, and a faint mono line `.doc-base` "based on 603d2e6". Save calls `DOCS.save(path, text)`. On ok the view returns to read mode with the new text and a quiet muted line under the head, `.doc-saved`: "Saved: 7a1c0d4 on master · 14:12". On a refusal the editor stays, with `.doc-refused` in the amber text colour above the row: "The file changed since you opened it (Sebastian, 14:08). Your text is kept." and a link "show theirs" `[data-act="theirs"]` that toggles a read-only pane with the current text under the editor. The draft survives a reload: keep the editor's text in `sessionStorage` under the path while editing, restore it on load.
4. **New document.** `[data-act="new"]` opens a small inline form in the head's place: `input.doc-name` with the placeholder "goal", a "Create" button, Escape cancels. Create opens `DOCS.newPath(name)` in edit mode with `# <Name>\n\n` as the text; the first save creates the file.
5. **Pins.** Where the variant has pins: a row `.doc-pins` under the head with one `button.doc-pin[data-doc]` per `DOCS.pins`, labelled by `DOCS.label`, `aria-current="page"` on the open one, and "+ pin" `[data-act="pin"]` that pins the open document for the session.
6. **The long read.** A small "Wide" button `[data-act="wide"]` in the head makes `#doc-view` cover the board's full width (the chat is never covered), the measure stays at 70ch and centres; the button reads "Narrow" `[data-act="narrow"]` while wide. No wide button on the phone.
7. **The phone.** At 390 the view is full screen like the base's drawer, one column, every target 44 px, no horizontal scroll, the edit row's buttons at full width. Say in your report how the moving between documents reads on the phone.
8. **The card drawer.** A card still opens in the base's drawer. What happens to the open document then is your variant's call (it closes, it stays behind, it stacks): say it in the report.
9. **Hooks.** `#doc-view`, `.doc-path`, `[data-doc]`, `[data-nav="back"]` and `[data-nav="forward"]`, `[data-mode]`, `#doc-editor`, `[data-act="save|cancel|new|pin|wide|narrow|theirs"]`, `.doc-saved`, `.doc-refused`, `.doc-pin`, `.doc-tab`, `.doc-crumb`, `.doc-peek`, `#doc-tree`, `.doc-recent`. Every new class starts with `doc-`, so the check can measure its contrast.
10. **Tokens only.** No new colour. Every new text pair at 4.5:1 or better in both themes on the surface it sits on. The light theme is the cream day version, never an inversion.

## The three variants

Filled in after the critics' ranking of the directions. Each variant's section names its direction images, its shape, its hooks beyond the shared ones, and its question.

Three variants, each a different way to hold and move between documents. All three carry the peek of direction 07 as a shared part, since both critics asked for it as the supplement to any shape:

11. **The peek.** Hovering or focusing a `doc-link[data-doc]` in the text for 400 ms shows a popover `.doc-peek` beside it in the console's panel style: the target's title in the text colour, `DOCS.firstParagraph(path)` in the muted colour over at most four lines, and a small "open ›" link `[data-doc]` at its foot. It closes on leaving the link or the popover, on Escape, and on a click anywhere else. No peek on a touch screen (no hover) and none on the phone. The peek is a glance, so it never steals focus and the page under it never scrolls.

### wiki (direction 01, with 07)

One document in the base's drawer slot: `#doc-view` is a panel docked at the right edge over the board exactly like `#drawer` (`position: absolute; right: 0; width: min(46%, 720px)`), so the board's Needs you column stays visible at its left and the chat is never covered. The head's first two controls are the history arrows: `‹` `[data-nav="back"]` and `›` `[data-nav="forward"]`, each disabled (`aria-disabled`, the faint colour) when there is nothing that way, then a "Recent ▾" menu `.doc-recent` listing `DOCS.nav.recent()` with `[data-doc]` rows, then the path, Read | Edit, Open in Forgejo, Wide, New document, ×. Under the head the pins row. Every `[data-doc]` opens in place through `DOCS.nav.open`; the view pushes `#doc=<path>` with `history.pushState` and listens to `popstate`, so the browser's back works as the arrow does, and a document's scroll position is restored on the way back. Opening a card closes the document (the drawer takes the slot), and the arrow's history survives: the next `?doc` or pin reopens where it was. Your question: with ten documents opened, do the arrows, the Recent menu and three pins carry the navigation without a tab strip, and does the way back read without being explained?

### tabs (direction 02)

The same docked panel as wiki, but the head's first row is a tab strip `.doc-tabs` with one `button.doc-tab[data-doc]` per opened document, labelled by `DOCS.label`, `aria-selected` on the open one, a small × on each (`[data-close-tab]`), and a `+` at the end that opens the New document form. Following a link opens its target as a new tab at the right and switches to it; a link to a document already open switches to its tab. A card opened from the board becomes a tab too (`.doc-tab.doc-tab-card`, "#612" with its state dot), and switching to it shows the base's drawer content inside the same panel. Under the strip the document's own row: the path, Read | Edit, Open in Forgejo, Wide, ×. No pins, no history arrows: the strip is the history. The strip is one line: beyond what fits, the tabs shrink to their first word, then the rest move into a "n more ▾" menu `.doc-tabs-more` at the strip's end. Build the state with six open documents reachable through `?doc=...&open=docs/layers.md,docs/architecture.md,docs/plans/2026-10-13-main-chat-docs.md,docs/board/roadmap.md,docs/target-architecture.md` so the limit shows. Your question: at 46 percent of the width, is the strip honest at six tabs, and is a card as a tab right or wrong?

### split (direction 04)

`#doc-view` takes the board's full width over the board (the chat never covered): two panes side by side, `.doc-pane.doc-pane-left` and `.doc-pane.doc-pane-right`, split by a hairline, each with its own small head (the path in mono, Read | Edit, Open in Forgejo) and its own body. The left pane opens the `?doc` document; a `[data-doc]` link clicked in the left pane opens its target in the right pane, and a link clicked in the right pane replaces the right pane; the clicked link in the left pane carries `aria-current="true"` and a faint accent background while its target is open at the right. Over both panes one row: "Swap ⇄" `[data-act="swap"]`, "One pane" `[data-act="one"]` (closes the right pane and gives the left the width, the button then reads "Two panes"), New document, ×. Pins sit in that row at the left and open into the left pane. The Wide button does not exist here (the view is already wide); the phone shows one pane, with a small "‹ roadmap" link `[data-nav="back"]` at the top of the right pane's content to return. Since the board is covered, under the top row runs one faint mono line `.doc-needs` "Needs you 2 · Merge PR #612 · #603 Which store for the session table? · show board" where each card ref is a `[data-card]` opener and "show board" `[data-act="board"]` closes the view. Your question: at 1440 the two panes are about 430 px each, a measure near 55 characters; does the side-by-side read pay for the lost board, and would the split be better as a Wide state of the wiki variant than as a shape of its own?
