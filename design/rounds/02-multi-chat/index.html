Console design round 02: the multi-chat console

The UX round of the autonomy and multi-chat plan, roadmap 239, on the Mission control shell as the console has it on 2026-10-14. The brief names the eight things the round designs: the thread switcher, the unread badge, the working mark, the inbox thread, the escalation's face, archive and delete, the commands, the autonomy level selector. Eight directions as images, the critic's ranking, three variants as built prototypes with the five scenarios of the brief, shot in both themes at 1440 and on the phone at 390, checked and measured. The notes hold the run. Sebastian picks.

The directions

Eight directions in both themes from one prompt head (the fixed shell) and one content block (the threads), differing in where the switcher and the level selector live: a rail, tabs, a title that opens the list, a dock of chips above the composer, a command palette, a mono table with lamps in the strip, a messenger list, the inbox as its own region. Gemini 2.5 Pro ranked them (the prompt, the verdict): instrument 9, rail 8, tabs 6, inbox split 5, messenger 5, command 4, title menu 2, dock 2. The critic carried instrument, rail and messenger, and the director agreed: tabs and dock scroll sideways on the phone, title menu and command hide the other threads' state, inbox split spends a third of the chat's height on a thread that is quiet most of the day.

What every variant shares

One chat engine in all three. The threads sort by last turn with the inbox pinned first, the archived last under a rule, dimmer, each with restore. The open thread is marked at the row's left edge. A thread with a turn in flight carries a breathing cyan dot; a thread with turns the member has not seen carries an amber count, and the browser tab reads "(n) Factory console". Opening a thread clears its badge; reading another thread never clears the inbox's. The inbox is the factory's thread, named "Inbox" with a house mark; a poke arrives in it as a turn by "the factory", one per state change, with choices as buttons where a decision fits, and with its decider line ("assisted level · started by the agent") when the agent acted on its own. An escalation is a poke marked "needs you" with an amber rule; its choices sit in an answer row beside the text box, once, with "or in your words below". Archive hides and keeps. Delete asks: the warning names the thread, its message count, that the rows go for good, and that one event names the member, the thread and the count. "/" at the start of the text box opens a palette with /compact and /new; both also sit as chips under the box. A level change opens a confirm that says it writes a policy change into the audit and what the new level means. Every text pair at 4.5:1 or better in both themes, measured.

Compared, the critic picks messenger: "the most context at a glance through its two-line rows", "translates most effectively to mobile". It ranks instrument second for the idiom and names the table's phone risk, which the built phone shot answers, and rail last as "overly simple" with the level "as a tactical, per-chat toggle". On the level's placement the critic is of two minds across the round: over the directions it preferred the instrument strip, compared it prefers the top bar; both times it rejects the chat's head. It asks messenger to borrow instrument's text buttons for Archive and Delete and its mono alignment, and to harden the round marks. The craft notes of the single critiques, taken: the thread head's title now ends in an ellipsis, the rows gained 2 px, the answer buttons wrap. Not taken: more padding in the prototype's own chat bubbles, which the round does not touch.

The recommendation

Messenger's rows with instrument's lamps. The newest line is the one thing a member cannot get from a one-line row: what happened, before opening. The inbox row then reads "Merge PR #612 waits for you: high risk, one migration" under its badge, which is the poke itself. The lamps are the better face of the level than a select: the four positions stay visible, the lit one states the policy beside the project's other facts, and nothing can change by a stray scroll on a select. The strip has room for them at 1440 and stacks them at 390 without a second place. The thread's actions can stay behind the ⋯: Archive and Delete are rare, and two text buttons on every thread head make the rare case loud. If one variant as built: messenger.
