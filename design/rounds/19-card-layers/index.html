Round 19: the card's layers

This round designs the small card and the opened card for the plan "Every card readable". A card must tell a human who dropped in cold what it is, why it exists and where it stands, in layers. The goals and the card model are in the problem document, sections 5 and 6.

The round drew four directions, A to D, with the same cards and the same words. Each direction shows five frames: a work card in Queued, two cards in Needs you, three Done cards, a routine card, and the opened card #1850. Each frame shows at 1280 px in the dark theme and at 390 px in the light theme. Two image models drew every frame. Gemini 2.5 Pro and GLM 5V Turbo judged both drawings. The brief names what is fixed. The words are in prompts.py. The director's notes are in notes.md.

Pick by letter: A, B, C or D.

The director recommends A, the stack. Both critics ranked A first in both loops. Each layer has its own line: the ref and the state on top, then the title, the why line and the place line. A human who knows the work reads the title and stops. A human who dropped in cold reads three more short lines. The suggested answer is a full-width amber button, the loudest thing on the board.

The runner-up is C, one paragraph. Both critics ranked C second in both loops. A work card in C is one line shorter, but its title runs into its why line, so a human must read into the paragraph to find where the title ends.

Sebastian's pick becomes a C-entry that amends C11. ia.md is rewritten after the pick.

The critics' ranking

A ranks first and C second in all four answers. Each critic scored G3 (layers), G8 (one decision) and the whisper rule from 1 to 10. The table shows the sum, at most 30. Loop 1 judged the gpt-image drawing. The compare loop judged the nano-banana drawing of the same directions.

The reasons, in short:

  • A wins because each layer has its own line, so a human can stop at the title or read on. Its full-width button makes the one decision clear. Both critics scored it 8 to 10 on every rule.
  • C is quiet on normal cards. Three of the four answers scored its whisper 9 or 10. Running the title into the why line makes the title harder to find at a glance. Its compact button is quieter than A's.
  • B's plan rail adds a line of chrome to every quiet card. In the compare loop both critics found "+1" on the split button unclear.
  • D's labels make every card speak, so the ask stands out less. The labels also take width from the narrow Queued column.

The critics disagree on two points. In loop 1 GLM proposed B's split button for A. In the compare loop GLM backed A's single button, as Gemini did. Gemini put D third in loop 1 and B third in the compare loop. GLM put B third in both loops. GLM scored C one point above A in loop 1 and still named A the winner, for its mono place line and its hairlines.

What a builder must get right in A, from the compare loop: the gap in weight between a normal card and a card that needs a human (Gemini), and "suggested by" in a small grey type that never competes with the button (GLM). The director checked one more point: in the light theme the amber text takes the theme's #8a4f00, which measures 6.5:1 on the card, not the lighter amber the image model drew.

The director overruled eight claims, among them "C shows twice as many cards" and "B's rail shows on Done cards". The list is in notes.md. The critics' full answers: Gemini loop 1, GLM loop 1, Gemini compare, GLM compare.

Method and cost

A probe drew A's board frame on four models. google/nano-banana-2.1 at 2K and openai/gpt-image-2.5 at 1536 by 1024 drew all 16 frames each. blackforestlabs/flux-3 drew a legible probe in a grey theme, and alibaba/qwen-image-3 refused a prompt over 5,000 characters. The probe is in probe/. The first nano-banana pass of three A frames wrote part names onto the screen. They ran again with a fixed prompt, and the first pass is in first-pass/. Each image has a sidecar JSON with its model, its exact prompt, its parameters and its cost. The prompt files beside the images hold the final prompt of each frame.
