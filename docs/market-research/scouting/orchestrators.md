# Scouting: end-to-end orchestrators

Scout run 2026-09-29. Angle: systems that take an issue, a ticket, a spec or an alert and drive coding agents through to a pull request, a merge or a deploy, with their own task state. This covers projects that call themselves a "software factory" or "dark factory", background-agent platforms, issue-to-PR bots, Kubernetes-native coding-agent runners, "AI dev team" or "AI company" orchestrators, agent-fleet control planes, ports of OpenAI Symphony, and the internal platforms big companies have written up (Stripe, Ramp, Uber, Shopify, Coinbase). Everything on the seed list in [../README.md](../README.md) is left out (Symphony, OpenHands, Coder, Factory, Devin, Copilot, gh-aw, Paperclip, Gas Town, Codegen, Tembo, Ona, Jules, Cursor, Terragon, Charlie, Blitzy, Warp Oz, Sweep, Kilo, Continue, Rovo, Conductor, Claude Squad, Crystal, Vibe Kanban, kagent and the others).

Column notes: **Rel** is a 1 to 5 guess at how much the project resembles or informs our factory (5 = same loop, same concerns). **OSS** is yes, no, or "src-avail" for source-available licences such as BUSL, Polyform or custom. **Life** gives GitHub stars / creation date / last push, or the latest public date for closed products. Most GitHub numbers look high for the repositories' age, and the 2026 agent-tooling space is full of star-inflated repositories, so treat star counts as a rough signal only.

## Observations for the synthesis

- "Software factory" became a crowded label in 2026. Factory.ai (seed) owns it commercially; StrongDM's manifesto (Feb 2026), Cole Medin's repository, Vercel's eve template, Machinist, ESF, Fabro, Fusion, human, Loki Mode, Addy Osmani and a dozen small repositories all use it. Our positioning needs more than the name: re-seedability, policy outside the model, and task-scoped identity are rare in this list.
- The nearest open comparators to our whole loop are ESF (Temporal plus microVM plus egress boundary plus quarantine), Kelos and Optio (Kubernetes-native ticket to PR), Agyn and Centaur (Kubernetes plus a credential boundary plus spend caps), and Open-Inspect (opencode harness). None of them does all of the following together: a per-task projected ServiceAccount identity verified by a gateway, hard budget holds, a supervisor model, and auto-merge by risk rule.
- Ideas to take: StrongDM holdout scenarios stored outside the repository; FactoryKit and human's credential injection at egress with "the agent never touches git"; Fabro's per-node model stylesheet with fallbacks; Archon and Stripe blueprints mixing deterministic and agentic nodes; Cyrus streaming agent activity into the issue; AO's and Multica's live kanban with blockers; Loki Mode's offline-verifiable completion receipts; Fluent's traces-as-intake for self-improvement.
- Enterprise references for the portfolio story: Stripe Minions (1,300 PRs a week), Ramp Inspect (more than 50 % of PRs, opencode plus Temporal), Uber (more than 70 % of PRs agent-attributed), Shopify River, Coinbase Forge. All keep a human merge gate except StrongDM.
