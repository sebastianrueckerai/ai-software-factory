## Notes for the synthesis

- **Replay and trace UI.** Nobody offers a self-hosted, forge-agnostic per-task replay joined to cost and PRs, except LangWatch. The small open viewers (agent-session-replay, Claude Trace Replay, Mindwalk) are design references only. agent-session-replay's ranked findings mirror our supervisor detectors.
- **Evaluation.** Harbor is the obvious base: its task directory format (instruction, environment, verifier) could be generated from our own closed issues plus their reproduction gates, which would give us success rate per tier and a harness regression suite in one move. Research notes from the searches: HarnessTax (Berkeley Sky Lab and Arena.ai, 2026-09-16) found that the harness moves success by a few points but cost by up to 5x. Scrouting (arXiv 2608.04804) found that the tasks weaker models solve are mostly a subset of what the strongest model solves, so routing should predict "cheap suffices" rather than look for complementary skills. CodeRescue (arXiv 2607.19338) frames what to do after a cheap model fails as a choice between recovery actions, not always "use a stronger model". All three matter for a learned model ladder.
- **Telemetry-to-fix.** Latitude (open source) and Temps (open source, self-hosted) are the two open implementations. Both keep humans on merge. Temps's two-phase analyze-then-fix approval and Rollbar's rule-based trigger are the UX patterns to copy.
- **Merge gates.** Orbi and the merge-gate repository both insist the verdict is bound to the exact head SHA and the branch is current with base. Worth checking that our `auto` rule does the same.
- **Mobile.** Happy (E2E encrypted relay), Superset (iPhone diff review) and CC Pocket (approval buttons on the session list) show the UX. None fits a server-rendered htmx console directly, but the "approve from the list" pattern does.
