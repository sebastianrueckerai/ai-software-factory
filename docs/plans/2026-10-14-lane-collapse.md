# Plan: no card is filed undecided — the lanes collapse

Sebastian's decisions of 2026-10-14, from the flow discussion in the controller chat: chat shapes work, the roadmap holds intentions, the board shows commitments. The plan-or-direct decision moves to the moment of filing: whoever files a card sets its track (plan or direct) there, because that is where the context is richest, and no card ever waits for an admission decision. A queue holds work waiting for capacity, never work waiting for a decision. The three lanes collapse: the backlog dies (the roadmap holds interest, ordered by Priority — item 205's ruled split), the plan lane becomes the card's track (planning is work), the ready lane becomes the queued status. The board keeps its five status columns and loses its decision buttons. One scheduler pulls the next committed card when a slot frees, with a slow sweep as the failsafe. This is a process-schema change and it lands as one round with the pull, so no committed card strands between the old admission and the new flow; the buttons do not survive an interim. Sources: the flow discussion of 2026-10-14, items 205, 219, 224, ruling 255, [layers.md](../layers.md), Stage 18's cost record.

## What exists

- The board's five columns map status, never lanes (ruling 255): Needs you, Queued, Working, Verifying, Done (board.go, fiveTitles). Beside them the console renders the process lanes (backlog, plan, ready) as columns with the decision buttons: moveCard into a starting lane (the Plan and Ready buttons), startPlan for an accepted plan's backlog cards, acceptPlan as the approver's click.
- A human-filed card parks in the backlog undecided; a move admits it, gated by the lane's moved_by (writers or approvers) through MayMove. Ready files the task queued with the ready lane's class, and the lease admission starts its session. The plan lane runs the planner; its accepted children wait in the backlog until Start plan moves them into ready.
- The machine sources never park: alerts, the roller and the template pass file decided cards. Finding 224 names the factory-PR sources' entry, its fix by construction written.
- The plan machinery: the planning session (the plan class), plan questions to the writers, change requests and rejection, the stale-revision check, max_cards 2 to 20, and "plans do not nest".
- Dependencies are task properties (wait_until done, on_failed human); today they hold the plan's children in lane ready.
- The spend brakes exist and are measured: the WIP bound, the project budget, the session caps (Stages 16 to 18: planned cards at 0.019 to 1.24 EUR against Stage 16's four budget refusals in seven unplanned cards).
- Item 205 (planned) gives the main loop the promotion: a roadmap item is promotable without asking when its Priority is filled and a plan or concept doc backs it; its phase two names the auto-pick when slots free.

## Work items

1. **Track on the task, set by the filer.** A track field on the task, plan or direct, required at filing; the schema refuses an undecided filing, so no source can produce one. The chat's new_card carries the track with a one-line reason; the manual form carries the choice with direct as its default; the alert, roller, template, roll and factory-PR sources keep their decided entries (224's fix gives the factory-PR sources the roll's entry). A plan-track card takes the plan class; the plan bounds (max_cards) move from lanes.plan to the track's config in the process file.

2. **The lanes block goes.** The process file loses lanes, moved_by and the starting-lane validation; backlog, plan and ready leave the lane vocabulary. What each carried moves: admission permission becomes the promotion bounds (the chat files as the member; who may file what is policy per source); dependency waiting moves to queued tasks (wait_until already sits on the task); the plan's bounds attach to the track. The code follows: LaneNames, laneTitles, moveCard, startPlan, acceptPlan and MayMove's lane questions go, and `factoryd config check` refuses a process file that still carries a lanes block — a removed schema, not an ignored one.

3. **The board shows status only.** The lane columns and the Plan, Ready, Start and accept buttons go; the five status columns stay. The card face names the current work stage from the session kind (planning, building, verifying). Needs you means a person is the next actor and nothing else: the project's humans, and the waiting-on-author exception is dropped (219's ruling already names the column's rule: static cards whose one remaining action is a human click). No ordering UI: the queue's order is filing order, and the roadmap's Priority order governs promotion (item 205).

4. **The plan flows without clicks.** A plan-track card runs its planning session. Open questions surface in Needs you (the existing question machinery) and an answer resumes the card. A plan with no open questions files its children and the planning card closes done in the same step: the accept click and the Start plan click both go. The children are ordinary cards: direct track, their brief their slice of the plan, the plan linked as artifact, dependencies from the plan, committed the moment the plan completes, never a plan child ("plans do not nest" survives as: a plan-track card files direct children only). A child that fails, or whose work shows the plan wrong, raises a question like any card: the plan is a document, not a contract.

5. **One scheduler: pull on slot vacate, sweep as failsafe.** One admission step owns every start: when a task leaves working, its slot frees and the next committed task starts, in filing order; one slow sweep re-derives the same answer as the failsafe for a dead session, a missed event, a crashed factoryd (the gatewatch pattern). The WIP bound, the project budget and the session caps sit at the pull; the admission click's spend brake is replaced by them. This step is the executor of 205's phase-two auto-pick, which it refines: promotion files commitments any time, and the bound binds the pull, not the filing. No cron: the periodic checkpoint card stays the layers doc's open primitive, and the main loop's own heartbeat is the item after 205.

## Not in this round

Board ordering UI (dragging or reordering), epic or grouping views for plans, the periodic checkpoint card, the durable main-loop agent and its heartbeat (the item after 205), the notification service (145), multi-member authorship rules on Needs you, risk classes per project (the layers doc's open point).
